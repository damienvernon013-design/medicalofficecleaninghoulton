How Often Should a Medical or Dental Office Be Professionally Cleaned?

Patient volume, exam room count, and facility type all factor into the right frequency.

The General Rule for Medical Facilities

Most medical and dental practices need daily cleaning of exam rooms, operatories, and waiting areas — the patient-contact intensity of a clinical environment is higher than a standard office, and infection-control expectations follow accordingly.

Factors That Determine Frequency

  • Patient volume: higher daily patient counts mean more surface contact and faster turnover requirements between exam rooms.
  • Facility type: urgent care and primary care with high daily volume typically need daily disinfection; lower-volume specialty practices may run a three-day schedule.
  • Operatory or exam room count: more rooms in active rotation means more disinfection cycles per day, independent of total square footage.
  • Seasonal factors: cold and flu season is a reasonable trigger to temporarily increase high-touch surface disinfection frequency.

Frequency by Facility Type

  • Primary care / urgent care: daily exam room and waiting room disinfection.
  • Specialty clinics (lower volume): three to five days per week, depending on patient count.
  • Dental practices: daily operatory cleaning, tied to your appointment schedule rather than a fixed calendar slot.

Don't Skip Periodic Deep Cleans

Regardless of your routine schedule, periodic deep cleaning of floors and hard-to-reach areas keeps the facility from degrading between routine visits. Most providers offer this as an add-on for existing clients.
