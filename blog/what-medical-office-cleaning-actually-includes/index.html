What Does Medical Office Cleaning Actually Include?

Not every provider includes the same tasks — here's what to expect and what to ask about.

Standard Inclusions

  • Exam room disinfection: exam tables, high-touch surfaces, and adjacent counters, using EPA List N-registered products with correct dwell times.
  • Waiting room and reception cleaning: chairs, armrests, door handles, check-in counters, and payment terminals.
  • Restroom sanitization: full fixture cleaning, disinfection, and restocking of consumables.
  • Floor cleaning: vacuuming and mopping with cross-contamination controls between clinical and non-clinical zones.
  • Trash removal: all bins emptied and relined, including biohazard-adjacent areas (not regulated waste disposal itself).

Common Add-Ons

  • Dental operatory-specific protocols and sterilization-room-adjacent cleaning
  • Floor stripping, waxing, or carpet extraction
  • Post-construction or renovation cleanup
  • Terminal cleaning after a known contamination event
  • Exterior window washing

What to Ask Your Provider

Ask for a written scope of work rather than a verbal agreement, and confirm which disinfectants are used and whether staff are trained in bloodborne pathogen protocols. See our page on EPA List N disinfectants for what that certification actually means.

Customization Is Standard

A good provider tailors the scope to your specific layout — a dental practice with a sterilization room, an urgent care with a triage area, or a multi-specialty clinic with varied patient flow all need a scope built around their actual space, not a generic package.
